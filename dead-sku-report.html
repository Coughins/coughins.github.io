What we found

The finding

A product whose every variant is unavailable does not quietly disappear. Its page stays live and indexed, it keeps absorbing crawl budget, it keeps receiving ad clicks, and it keeps being submitted to Google Shopping and Meta catalogues — where it is suppressed or disapproved rather than sold. Shoppers who land on it bounce.

This is not a rare failure. 32 of the 34 stores we scanned were carrying dead products, and 11 of them were carrying enough to affect a meaningful share of their catalogue. The worst in the sample, intelligentsia.com, had 234 of 331 published products (70.7%) with nothing buyable on the page. The median store scored 80 out of 100.

These are competent, well-run brands. That is the point: the problem accumulates silently because nothing in Shopify surfaces it, and the public feed is the one place it becomes measurable from outside.

How it works

Send a store URL. Everything is read from that store's own public /products.json feed — the same file Google Shopping, Meta catalogues and every marketplace importer already read. There is no login, no app install, no code on your site, and nothing touching order or customer data. robots.txt is checked and respected before the first request, and a store that disallows it is skipped rather than worked around.

A scan takes a few seconds. The audit comes back as a single self-contained HTML file you can open or forward, plus the CSVs.

Method. Each store's public product feed was paginated at 250 products per request with a delay between pages, capped at 2,500 products per store. "Dead" means a published product where every variant reports available: false. Availability is what the feed reports at scan time. Scores weight each check by the share of the catalogue it affects and cap each check's penalty, so no single issue can alone zero a score.

Limits. Snapshots, not trends — a store scanned on a restock day looks different. The audit reads feed structure, not merchandising intent, so it produces a list of things worth checking rather than a list of mistakes. Stores that block or do not expose the public feed were excluded, not penalised.
